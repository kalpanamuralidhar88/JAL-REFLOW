The problem

Understanding textile water waste

Textile wet processing moves cloth and yarn through washing, preparation, dyeing and rinsing. Each step uses water, and each step leaves behind wastewater with a different composition.

Water goes in at every stage

Preparation, dyeing and rinsing all draw water. Rinsing after dyeing is repeated until the cloth is clean, so a lot of water leaves the unit with relatively little in it.

Wastewater varies by step

A scouring bath, a dye bath and a final rinse do not carry the same load. Treating all of them as one stream hides what could be recovered.

ColourSuspended matterDissolved saltsOrganic contaminants

Recoverable rinse water is lost

Final-rinse water is usually the cleanest stream a unit produces. Mixed with stronger streams, or simply discharged, its reuse potential is lost.

Interactive

From problem to opportunity

Space is limited

Small units rarely have room for a large conventional treatment plant with several tanks and a dedicated operating area.

Cost is a barrier

Large arrangements are built for scale. For a small producer the capital and running cost can be hard to justify.

Streams should be separated

Collect suitable rinse streams on their own and treat them for what they still contain, instead of treating everything the same way.

Our solution

JAL REFLOW: a compact, modular treatment tower

A floor-standing, rectangular, vertical tower that receives separately collected final-rinse water, treats it in stages according to what it still contains, checks the result and sends it towards reuse only when it passes.

Status labels used on this site:CompletedIn developmentProposed · needs validation

Sludge management

Coagulation produces a settled sludge that carries the removed solids. It is drawn off from the bottom of the settling chamber and collected separately from the treated water. Handling and disposal route depend on the coagulant used and on what the water carried, and need to be checked against local requirements.

Spent-media management

Where activated carbon, biochar or polishing media are used, they eventually lose capacity. The design aims for removable media sections so spent material can be replaced without dismantling the tower, then handled as a separate waste stream.

Technology and engineering

One tower, built to be operated and serviced locally

Select a markerTap a numbered marker on the tower to see what that section does.
Source-segregated rinse-water recovery
Only separately collected final-rinse water enters the tower. Keeping this stream apart from stronger bath discharges is what makes recovery realistic for a small unit.
Modular vertical architecture
Treatment steps are stacked as compartments in one rectangular tower, so each step has a defined place and can be inspected, changed or replaced on its own.
Compact floor-standing installation
The tower uses floor area for a single column instead of a row of tanks, which suits workshops with limited space.
Removable and replaceable components
The 100-mesh nylon filter, media sections, valves and fittings are designed to be taken out and replaced without special tools.
Gravity flow as a design objective
Water enters at the top and moves downward from stage to stage. Whether gravity alone gives a reliable flow through every stage is a design objective that still has to be validated.
Batch or continuous operation
The tower may run in batches or continuously. Which one works better depends on validation with real rinse water and is not yet decided.
Quality-based secondary treatment
After primary treatment the remaining contaminants are assessed. Activated carbon or biochar, polishing filtration or pH correction are added only when the water needs them.
Process-specific reuse verification
Water is released towards reuse only when it meets the acceptance requirements of the process it is meant for, such as scouring.
Local materials and maintenance access
The design favours materials and parts that are easy to source locally, so a small unit is not tied to a distant supplier for routine upkeep.
Where we aim to be different. Coagulation, filtration and adsorption are established treatment processes and we do not claim to have invented them. Our aim is an integrated, affordable and application-specific package for small textile units: sized for them, serviced locally, and checked against the needs of a specific reuse.

Experimental development

Testing with real textile final-rinse water

An initial experimental investigation into recovering final-rinse wastewater from a textile-processing unit.

Completed

Sample and approach

The team obtained a small quantity of actual wastewater from a textile-processing unit. The sample was final-rinse wastewater, collected specifically as a rinse stream and not as general untreated industrial wastewater.

The team then carried out experimental treatment on this collected sample to investigate how it could be treated and whether it could be recovered.

Completed

Treatment operations carried out

The sample was taken through a sequence of treatment operations that mirrors the stages of the JAL REFLOW tower.

ScreeningPhysical separation of fibres and larger suspended particles.
SettlingGravity separation of heavier solids from the water.
Coagulation / flocculationDestabilising fine colloidal particles so they gather into flocs that can be separated.
Chemical treatmentChemical treatment of the sample as part of the primary treatment step.
PolishingA final filtering step on the treated water.
Observed

What we observed

The treated sample showed visible improvement and approximately neutral pH.

These observations indicate an initial improvement in the visible characteristics and the pH of the sample. They are the result of a first experimental investigation.

Still to be established

What these results do not yet show

  • Complete removal of contaminants.
  • Quantitative removal efficiency for any parameter.
  • Any reduction in dissolved salts.
  • That the water is suitable for scouring. This needs process-specific acceptance testing.

The next step is a structured assessment of the treated water against the parameters listed under final water quality verification: pH, TDS, electrical conductivity, turbidity, suspended solids and colour.

In development

Towards a working tower

The tower described on this site is the engineering concept that these experiments feed into. Its stages, flow behaviour and operating mode are being developed and still need validation.

Future vision

Beyond Water Recovery: The Next Frontier.

Two independent research directions we want to explore later. Neither is a capability of JAL REFLOW today.

Future research direction

Algal-based treatment

We would like to study selected microalgae for taking up nutrients and for possible polishing of suitable, already pretreated wastewater, in a greenhouse-integrated environment (conceptual illustration above).

Cooling-water treatment and reuse

We would like to explore whether suitable cooling-water streams can be treated and reused in appropriate industrial applications (conceptual illustration above).

Each stream and each intended use would need its own quality and compatibility assessment before any reuse is considered.
